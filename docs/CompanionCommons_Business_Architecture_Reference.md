# CompanionCommons — Business Architecture Reference
**Source:** Shared by John, Aug 24 2026, as a working reference for how he's structuring the company across three streams.

## 1. The Customer Acquisition Engine
Website: Companion Commons
Companion Commons iOS/Android Applications, PWA
Companion Commons Community Facebook groups
Companion Commons: advertising, marketing, etc. to shelters, rescues, breed communities, micro influencers, paid acquisition, referral programs, etc.
Companion Commons social: Facebook, Instagram, Reddit, etc.

**Purpose:** get people to join a community changing the way we understand our pets' health.
**Output:** New pet owners + pets entering the platform.

## 2. The Pet Health Platform
**Purpose:** Give owners a reason to stay.

Pet Owner:
- Create a baseline/profile
- Baseline health assessment
- Weekly 30-second health check-in
- Supplement/medicine, mobility, energy, appetite, cognitive, weight tracking; dashboard, weekly tracker, graphs, breed guides, access to Facebook groups, etc.
- Personalized insights and comparisons to other similar breeds
- Rewards/gamification

**Core idea:** Make tracking your pet's health so easy and rewarding that owners actually keep doing it.

## 3. The Data Engine
This is ultimately the most valuable asset.

Inputs: Pet profile + Baseline health + Weekly outcomes + Products/supplements + Medications + Diet + Lifestyle + Adverse events + Time → Longitudinal Pet Health Dataset

## 4. Intelligence Layer
Turn raw data into something commercially valuable: health benchmarks, outcome trends, cohort analysis, product effectiveness signals, breed comparisons, age-related trends, condition trajectories, risk indicators, predictive models, AI-generated insights.

This is where you transition from "pet health app" to "Pet Health Intelligence Platform."

## 5. Enterprise Products
This is where the company ultimately makes significant money.

- **Pharma:** real-world evidence, product outcome studies, post-market surveillance, patient/owner insights
- **Pet Food:** product effectiveness, consumer outcomes, breed/age segmentation
- **Supplement Companies:** product efficacy, benchmarking, customer outcomes
- **Pet Insurance:** risk modeling, health trajectories, claims-related insights
- **Veterinary Networks:** patient monitoring, engagement, population health
- **Research Organizations / Universities:** research datasets, cohort recruitment, study infrastructure

## 6. Revenue Engine
Several layers rather than one revenue stream:

| Revenue Stream | Customer | Potential Model |
|---|---|---|
| Enterprise SaaS | Pharma, food, insurance | Annual subscription |
| Research Studies | Pharma / universities | Project fee |
| Data Licensing | Enterprise | Annual license |
| Benchmark Reports | Industry | Subscription/report |
| API | Technology companies | Usage-based |
| Custom Analytics | Enterprise | Project/annual |
| AI Models | Enterprise | Licensing/API |

## The most important structural insight (original framing)
The company has two businesses operating together:

**Business #1 — Consumer Acquisition** (Pet products + health platform): acquire and retain pet owners.
**Business #2 — Pet Health Intelligence** (Data + analytics + research infrastructure): monetize the resulting longitudinal dataset.

The consumer business doesn't need to generate huge margins by itself. Its strategic purpose: acquire pets → collect longitudinal outcomes → build proprietary dataset → generate intelligence → sell intelligence to enterprises.

## The 4 core systems
1. **Acquisition System** — How do we get pets?
2. **Engagement System** — How do we get owners to continuously provide data?
3. **Intelligence System** — How do we transform observations into useful intelligence?
4. **Monetization System** — Who pays, what do they buy, and why is it worth paying for?
